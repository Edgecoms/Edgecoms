Most people typing "elevar alternative" into Google are doing it for one of two reasons. Either the invoice got uncomfortable, or they noticed that getelevar.com now redirects somewhere unexpected and want to know what happened.

Both are fair. This is a comparison of five Shopify server-side tracking apps with live pricing, live ratings, and an honest read on which one suits which store. One of them is ours, and it is not the right answer for everyone.

## Why are people leaving Elevar?

Elevar was acquired by Buxton, and in 2025 the product was folded into Audiense. The Shopify listing is still live and still maintained, but the pricing page now reads <Cite href="https://www.audiense.com/products/audiense-online/pricing/">Audiense Online Pricing, Powered by Elevar</Cite>, and the old domain redirects there.

Nothing about that makes the software worse. It does change what you are buying into: a tracking tool inside a broader consumer-analytics platform, rather than a focused ecommerce product. Some merchants care about that and some do not.

The more common reason is simpler. Elevar's entry plan is <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/gtm-datalayer-by-elevar">$225 a month for up to 2,000 orders</Cite>. For a store doing 300 orders a month, that is a lot of money per order for a piece of plumbing.

That is worth working out for your own store before you shop at all, because the answer flips somewhere in the middle.

<Example>
A store doing 300 orders a month on Elevar Core pays $225, which is 75 cents an order.

The same store at 2,000 orders a month pays the same $225, which is about 11 cents an order.

At 30,000 orders on Premium it is $1,250, which is roughly four cents an order.

The price does not really rise with your business. It falls, per order, and quite steeply. Which means the merchants with the strongest case for leaving are the smallest ones, and the merchants who complain loudest about the invoice are often the ones for whom it has already become cheap.
</Example>

## Where is Elevar still the right answer?

It would be easy to leave this section out. It is also the section that decides whether the rest of this page is worth trusting.

Elevar is the deepest product of the five, and there are stores that should pay for it:

- **You run headless, or something unusual.** Elevar has spent years on the awkward implementations, and that experience is real. The cheaper apps mostly assume a standard Shopify storefront.
- **You need session stitching across visits.** Its listing leads on connecting events and sessions to recognise returning anonymous users, which is a harder problem than firing a purchase event and is not something a free app is likely to solve well.
- **You want checkout analytics in the same tool.** Fewer moving parts is worth money at some scale.
- **You are big enough that the price stops mattering.** At 30,000 orders a month, $1,250 is roughly four cents an order. Nobody switches tracking vendors to save four cents an order.

If any of those describe you, the honest recommendation is to stay where you are. Below roughly a thousand orders a month, the price is much harder to justify.

## Which one should you actually pick?

By situation, not by score:

- **You live in GA4 and want it right.** Analyzify. It has the most reviews of the five at <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/analyzify">272</Cite>, it is built around Google's stack, and its annual plans fold in the implementation fee rather than billing it separately.
- **You are small or seasonal.** Littledata's Flex plan. Paying per order with no monthly floor is the only pricing here that survives a quiet January.
- **You already run server-side GTM and want control.** Stape. If you are comfortable in a GTM container, a managed layer on top of it is unnecessary.
- **You want Meta, Google and TikTok conversions arriving correctly, and nothing else.** Trackproof. It does one job, and it is free.
- **You are complicated, or large, or both.** Elevar.

One thing not to do is pick on star rating. All five sit between 4.4 and 4.8, which is close enough to be noise, and the counts vary by a factor of seven. A 4.4 across 38 reviews and a 4.8 across 124 are not really comparable measurements, and neither tells you whether the app handles your particular theme, your particular checkout, or your particular destination list. Read the one-star reviews instead. They are where the specific failures live, and they are the only part of a listing that is not marketing.

## What should you check before you switch?

Price is the easiest thing to compare and the least important.

1. **Does it deduplicate against your existing pixel?** If the browser and the server both report a purchase and the platform cannot tell they are the same event, your reported revenue doubles and your decisions get worse. This is the single question that matters most.
2. **What does it do with consent?** An app that sends events for shoppers who declined marketing is not a feature, it is a liability with your name on it.
3. **What customer data does it send, and how is it normalised?** Match quality depends on the email being lowercased and trimmed and the phone being in E.164. Apps differ here more than they admit.
4. **Which destinations are supported?** Meta, GA4 and Google Ads are table stakes. TikTok, Klaviyo, Pinterest and Snapchat are not.
5. **What happens at your actual order volume in twelve months?** Every tier here has a cliff. Work out where yours is before you commit.
6. **Can you export or leave?** A tool that owns your event configuration is one you will be reluctant to test alternatives against.

A note on the free plans, since two of the five have one. Free tracking apps are usually free because the vendor sells something else, or because the free tier is a funnel into a paid one at volume. Neither is sinister, but it does mean you should check what the paid tier costs before you build a year of reporting on the free one. Ours is free because it is new and narrow, which is its own kind of trade.

## How do you switch without breaking your reporting?

The failure mode is not losing data. It is creating a discontinuity you cannot explain three months later.

1. **Record a baseline week.** Reported purchases per platform, next to Shopify's own order count.
2. **Install the new app without removing the old one.** Most of them will happily coexist for a few days.
3. **Watch for double counting immediately.** If reported purchases jump above your Shopify order count, the two apps are both reporting and not deduplicating. This is expected during the overlap and is exactly why you keep it short.
4. **Compare match quality after a few days**, not conversions. Conversions will look similar. Match quality is where apps separate.
5. **Turn the old one off, and write down the date.** When someone asks in November why the numbers stepped in August, that note is the answer.
6. **Leave it alone for a full attribution window** before judging anything.

## What none of these apps fix

Worth saying plainly, because every listing on this page is selling recovered conversions.

Server-side tracking closes the gap caused by blocked, expired and dropped requests. It does not create consent you were never given, it does not restore view-through attribution, and it will never make your ad platform numbers equal your Shopify numbers. Different attribution windows and different modelling guarantee a permanent gap. The realistic goal is a smaller, explainable gap. Anyone promising you a perfect match is selling something.

If the mechanics of that are new to you, the [full guide to server-side tracking](/blog/shopify-server-side-tracking) covers what breaks and why before you spend anything.
