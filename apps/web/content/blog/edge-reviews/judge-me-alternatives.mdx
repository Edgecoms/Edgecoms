You installed Judge.me because everyone said to, it worked, and it cost nothing. Then something changed. A line item appeared, or the one job you needed doing turned out to sit on the plan above yours, or you looked at your own product page and saw somebody else's brand sitting under your star rating.

That is what sends a merchant searching for a judge.me alternative. Almost never the software failing. The listing still carries <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/judgeme">5.0 stars across 43,884 reviews</Cite>, which is the strongest social proof any app in this category has.

This post is the money argument, not a feature bake-off. Where does the cheap default stop being cheap, and what does the price tag leave out.

## What does Judge.me's free plan actually cap you at?

Start with what it does not cap, because most comparison posts get this bit wrong.

The listing describes the free plan as permanent, with <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/judgeme">unlimited reviews and unlimited photo and video reviews</Cite>. No order band. No monthly quota that trips you into a paid tier the month a product goes viral. In a category where nearly every other vendor meters something, that is why the app ended up on so many stores.

So the ceiling is not volume. It is capability, and capability ceilings behave differently:

- **A volume cap is predictable.** You forecast the month you cross it and budget for it.
- **A capability cap is not.** You hit it the day a specific job appears, usually while you are busy with something else, so the decision gets made under pressure.
- **Capability caps move.** Vendors reshuffle what sits on which plan, so your forecast from a year ago is not evidence.

Open the pricing tab and read your own plan against the one above it. Not from memory, and not from a blog post, including this one.

If the free plan does everything you need, stop shopping. The cheapest software in any category is the software you already have working.

## At what monthly order volume does the paid plan stop being cheap?

On volume alone, it does not. That is the honest answer.

The Awesome plan is listed at <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/judgeme">$15 a month with a 15-day trial</Cite>. Flat, not order-banded, so the cost per order falls the whole way up your growth curve.

<Example>
A store doing 100 orders a month on a $15 plan pays 15 cents an order.

At 1,000 orders a month, the same $15 is about 1.5 cents an order.

Now run an order-banded competitor. A plan that includes 300 orders and adds $50 for each additional 300 lands near the flat plan at 300 orders. At 3,000 orders it does not. The flat plan is still $15.
</Example>

That is why the cheap-default reputation has held. It also says where to look instead, because if price per order is not what breaks, something else is:

- **Staff time.** Moderating, replying and chasing reviews is a person's hours. It scales with review volume, not with your plan.
- **Theme work.** Every widget you place, restyle or fix after a theme update is billed at developer rates.
- **Tools it does not replace.** If review requests go out through your email platform, that bill is part of your reviews bill.

Add those up for a month. On most stores the software line is the smallest number by a distance, so switching vendors to save it optimises the wrong column.

## Which Judge.me capabilities are paid add-ons rather than plan inclusions?

I will not list features from memory. Vendors move things between tiers, and a stale feature list in a blog post is how merchants buy the wrong plan. Here is the method instead, which survives the next repricing:

1. **Open the pricing tab on the listing.** It is the current version. Everything else is a copy of a copy.
2. **Write down the three jobs you need doing.** Not the twenty you might want. The three that made you open the tab.
3. **Find each one on the plan you are on.** If a job is not named there, treat it as absent, not as probably included.
4. **Check whether it is a separate app.** Some vendors ship adjacent capability as its own listing at its own price. Second install, second bill.
5. **Price the alternative to buying it.** Sometimes the answer is a spreadsheet and twenty minutes a week.

The costs that catch people are rarely on the pricing page anyway. These show up later:

| Cost | Where it lands | Rough shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moderation and replies | Staff hours | Grows with review volume, not plan tier |
| Widget placement and restyling | Developer time | One-off per theme, then again after each theme update |
| Review request sending | Your email platform bill | Grows with contacts, not with reviews |
| Migration between vendors | Your time plus risk | Paid once, felt for a fortnight |

That bottom row is the one people forget when they shop to save $15.

## Is the branded verified badge on your widget costing you anything?

This pushes more merchants off free plans than price ever does.

A third-party mark does two opposite things at once. It tells a shopper the reviews were collected by an independent system rather than typed in by you, which is worth something on a page where the store has an obvious interest in the outcome. It also puts a vendor's brand on your product page and gives a curious shopper somewhere to look other than your buy button.

Nobody can tell you from outside which effect wins, and I will not invent a number for it. How to price it:

- **If removing the badge is what a paid plan buys**, ask whether that is worth the fee alongside everything else on the plan. On a flat fifteen dollar plan it usually is, because you get the rest too.
- **If your brand is the product**, in fashion or beauty or anywhere the page design is the pitch, unbranded widgets are worth real money and you knew that already.
- **If you sell something technical or expensive**, the mark is doing useful work and removing it can cost more than it saves.
- **If you are unsure**, leave it. It is reversible.

Reviews pay for themselves when they sit where the doubt happens, which is a bigger lever than the badge. That is covered in the [Shopify product reviews guide](/blog/shopify-product-reviews).

## Which apps are a genuine Judge.me alternative, and on what basis?

The App Store lists dozens. Most are not real alternatives, because they meter in a way that punishes the exact store shape that made you look.

I only compare apps whose current pricing I have read myself, which leaves three other vendors. The fourth and fifth rows are Judge.me's own two plans. For a lot of stores the real decision is free plan against paid plan, not vendor against vendor.

| App or plan | Rating | Entry price | What that includes |

Read those entry prices as shapes rather than numbers. Judge.me is flat. Loox is banded and the bands are narrow, so the bill climbs in steps as you grow. Yotpo is free at very low volume and varies with order volume above it.

The basis for inclusion is simple. The app has to substitute for the whole job, and I have to see its current price. Anything I would guess at is not on the table.

## Does a store leaving over price want the same alternative as one leaving over support?

No. Conflating the two is how merchants end up switching twice.

**If you are leaving over price**, check what you actually pay first. Plenty of stores shopping for a replacement are on a free plan already, so the maximum saving is zero and the migration still costs a week. On the paid plan the saving is fifteen dollars a month. Weigh that against a fortnight of your attention.

**If you are leaving over support**, stop reading pricing tables. What matters is response time on your plan, whether a human answers, and whether the vendor will look at your theme when a widget breaks. A bigger vendor is not automatically better. Scale buys staffed hours. It also buys queues.

**If you are leaving over one missing capability**, that is the cleanest of the three, because you can test it before you commit.

## What do you lose in reviews, widgets and rankings the week you switch?

Less than you fear, but not nothing, and it lands in one week. Your reviews are content you own, and any serious vendor imports them. What you risk is the changeover, which is a presentation problem rather than a data one. Run it in this order:

1. **Install the new app without removing the old one.** Both coexist while nothing is published.
2. **Import, then check the totals on the review list.** Before you touch a single template.
3. **Place the new widget on one product template only.** Leave the rest alone.
4. **Compare it against a page still running the old widget.** Star average, review count, layout on mobile.
5. **Roll out to the remaining templates.** Product, collection cards, home page, wherever the old one appeared.
6. **Hunt for doubles.** Two widgets on one page is the commonest switching mistake and it is invisible from the admin.
7. **Wait a fortnight before uninstalling the old app.** Leaving a free app installed costs nothing and it is your rollback.

On rankings: search engines have to recrawl your product pages to see any change, so a differently rendered widget is never a same-day event either way. What hurts is a template where reviews silently stopped rendering and nobody noticed for a month. Step six is why.

## Where does Edge Reviews sit against Judge.me?

Straight answer: for most stores happy on Judge.me's free plan, it does not. Stay where you are.

Judge.me has a permanent free plan with no volume cap. If you are on it, it works, and nothing on your list of three jobs is missing, switching is a week of your life spent to change nothing.

Edge Reviews is new. It has no reviews on its listing yet and I will not pretend otherwise. It has a free plan and it imports your existing reviews, which matters, because an app that cannot take your history with it is not a real alternative to anything.

It is worth a look in the narrow case this post has circled. You are on an order-banded plan, the bands step up as you grow, and what you want is your reviews placed properly rather than another feature list. Install it on a development theme alongside what you have and compare the two on your own product pages.

If that is not you, the recommendation is the boring one. Judge.me free is hard to beat, because nothing is cheaper than free and nothing is safer than a setup that already works.
