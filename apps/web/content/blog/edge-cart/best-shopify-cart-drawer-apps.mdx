Your cart drawer is the last screen before checkout, and it is usually the least considered thing on the store. It got picked in an afternoon because it was the app with the most reviews, and now it loads a second script on every page and charges a monthly fee that climbs with your order count.

Most roundups of the best Shopify cart drawer apps hand you a feature grid where every app has every tick. That grid is useless. All of these apps do a slide-out cart, a free shipping bar, an upsell block and a note field. Knowing that tells you nothing about which one to install.

So this list is organised the other way round. Each app gets judged against one kind of store, and each entry says plainly who it is wrong for. If your store is not that profile, skip the section and keep moving.

## What separates a cart drawer app from a theme setting?

Modern Shopify themes already draw a drawer. Dawn does. Most paid themes do. What they give you is geometry: a panel that slides in, a list of line items, a quantity stepper and a checkout button. What they do not give you is logic.

That is the whole gap these apps sell into.

- **Conditional offers.** A theme can hardcode one recommended product. An app decides what to show based on cart contents, cart value, collection, or what is already in the basket, and it stops offering something the shopper has already added.
- **Progress state.** A theme can print a static "free shipping over 50" line. An app tracks the gap live, updates it as items go in and out, and can stack several thresholds in sequence.
- **Rules you can change without a developer.** Theme drawer logic lives in Liquid. Changing an offer means editing a file, which means a staging theme, which means it does not happen on a Tuesday afternoon.
- **Reporting on the cart itself.** Shopify analytics tells you about sessions and orders. It does not tell you how many people opened the drawer, saw an offer, and took it.

If none of those four sentences describe a problem you actually have, the honest answer is to keep your theme drawer. An app you configure once and never revisit is a monthly bill with a script attached.

## How were these apps judged, and on what evidence?

Everything checkable was checked. Everything that is not checkable is labelled as such. The process:

1. **Read the live App Store listing.** Star rating, review count, plan names, price per order band and trial length were pulled on 22 August 2026 and reproduced below without rounding.
2. **Install on a real theme.** Not a demo store. A drawer that behaves on clean Dawn can still fight a customised theme.
3. **Test the failure cases, not the happy path.** Empty cart, one item, twenty items, a sold-out variant, a discount code already applied.
4. **Refuse vendor performance claims.** No app here publishes an independent, reproducible benchmark. Any revenue lift figure a vendor quotes comes from their own dashboard, measured their own way.
5. **Name the weakness before the strength.** Every entry below has one stated plainly.

That last rule is why this post has no winner. A roundup that ranks one app first for every store profile is a sales page wearing a comparison table.

## Which app suits a single-product or one-SKU store?

This is the profile most badly served by the category, because almost every feature on offer assumes a catalogue.

If you sell one product in three sizes, there is no cross-sell to run. Your drawer has two jobs: show the quantity clearly, and get out of the way. What you might want beyond that is a quantity break, which is a bundle problem more than a cart problem. Read up on [product bundles](/blog/shopify-product-bundles) before you buy a cart app to solve it.

- **Amp Slide Cart** is the sensible pick here, because its Free Forever plan means a one-SKU store pays nothing for a drawer that looks better than the theme default. Amp holds 5.0 stars across 696 reviews <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/slide-cart">on the App Store</Cite>.
- **Edge Cart** also has a free plan and installs without touching theme files, which matters if your one-product store runs a bought theme you would rather not fork.
- **Upcart** is the wrong tool for this store. You would be paying from $29.99 a month <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/upcart-cart-builder">for the 0 to 200 order band</Cite> for rule engines that have nothing to decide between.

**The weakness:** on a single-SKU store, every app in this category is over-specified. You are buying a slide-out panel and leaving most of the product unused. That is fine when the price is zero and hard to justify when it is not.

## Which app suits a wide catalogue with frequent bundles?

Now the logic earns its keep. A store with 400 SKUs and a monthly bundle promotion needs the drawer to answer one question: given this cart, what is the single thing most worth offering?

**Upcart is the strongest fit.** It has the deepest rule surface of the three, the largest install base to argue it has been hit with strange edge cases already, and it holds 4.7 stars across 857 reviews <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/upcart-cart-builder">on its listing</Cite>. When your offer logic has genuine branching, that maturity is worth paying for.

<Callout title="Check the free plan wording before you rely on it" tone="warning">
Upcart's free tier is for development stores only <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/upcart-cart-builder">per its pricing page</Cite>. It is not a free plan for a live store, and merchants do get caught by that after building out a configuration on a dev store first.
</Callout>

Amp handles cross-sell too, and the question becomes how much rule nesting you need. If your offers are broad ("anyone over 60 sees the sample pack"), Amp's Pro plan at $29.99 a month <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/slide-cart">matches Upcart's entry band</Cite> and does the job. If your offers are per-collection with exclusions, Upcart holds up better.

**The weakness:** Upcart prices by order volume, so the app gets more expensive exactly as the store gets busier. At the 501 to 1000 order band it is $54.99 a month. Budget for the band above the one you are in.

## Which app suits a high-volume store watching every millisecond?

Here is the honest position: nobody in this category can prove they are the fast one, and you should distrust anyone who claims it.

What you can reason about is mechanism.

- **Theme edits versus app embeds.** An app that writes into your Liquid leaves residue and makes rollback a manual job. Edge Cart runs without theme edits, which is a maintenance argument as much as a speed one.
- **Sandbox context.** Shopify's Web Pixels API gives apps controlled APIs inside a Lax or Strict sandbox <Cite href="https://shopify.dev/docs/api/web-pixels-api">by design</Cite>, which is worth knowing when a cart app also claims to fire tracking events.
- **When the drawer markup renders.** A drawer built on open is cheaper on first paint than one rendered into the DOM on every page load.

The only trustworthy number is yours. Duplicate your live theme, install the app on the copy, and run the same product page three times with the app on and three times with it off, on a throttled mobile connection. Compare Largest Contentful Paint. Then uninstall the loser.

**The weakness:** every app here, mine included, asks you to take performance on faith. That is not good enough at high volume, and the test above takes twenty minutes.

## Which app suits a merchant who wants reward ladders more than a drawer?

Some merchants do not want a cart app. They want a ladder: spend 50 for free shipping, spend 90 for a sample, spend 150 for the gift. The drawer is only where the ladder gets shown.

**Amp is the closest fit** if the ladder is the point and the rest is decoration. Its free tier lets you build and watch the mechanic before committing, and the 7-day trial on paid plans <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/slide-cart">is short but sufficient</Cite> to see whether average order value moves.

**Upcart is the fit** if the ladder needs to differ by market, by collection, or by customer tag, and if you want the reward to be an automatically added line item rather than a discount code the shopper has to copy.

Be clear that a ladder is a margin decision. Every rung is revenue you have chosen to give back for a larger basket, and it only works if the uplift covers the giveaway.

<Example>
Say your average order is 62 and your gross margin is a little over half, so you keep about 34 per order. You add a free sample at 90 that costs you 4 landed. If a fifth of orders climb from 62 to 90, those orders now yield about 49.50 in margin minus the 4 sample, so 45.50. The other four fifths are unchanged, and some of them take the sample without spending more. Substitute your own margin and your own take-up rate before you build the ladder, because at a lower margin the same rung loses money.
</Example>

**The weakness:** a reward ladder is the feature most likely to be configured once, forgotten, and left promising a gift you stopped stocking in March. Whichever app you pick, put a calendar reminder on it.

## Where does Edge Cart sit in this list?

Edge Cart is our app, it is new, and it has no reviews yet. Ranking it first would be worthless, so here is the plain version.

It exists because of one specific complaint from stores we run: cart apps that edit the theme and leave debris behind, and pricing that scales with orders for a panel that does the same work at 200 orders as at 2,000. Edge Cart installs without theme edits and has a free plan.

Pick something else when:

- **You need deep, branching offer rules today.** Upcart has more surface and far more merchants who have already found its sharp edges.
- **You want proof before you install.** Amp has 696 reviews and a 5.0 rating. We have nothing yet, and that is a real reason to wait.
- **Your theme drawer is fine.** Then no app on this page is the answer, and keeping the script off the page is the better trade.

Pick Edge Cart when you want a drawer that does not touch your theme, on a free plan, from someone who will tell you when a competitor is the better call. That is the whole pitch. It is deliberately small.
