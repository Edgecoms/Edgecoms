You installed the Meta pixel, you turned on GA4, and the numbers still refuse to agree with your Shopify admin. So now you are reading about the best Shopify conversion tracking apps and trying to decide which one deserves a line on the invoice.

Most comparisons answer that with a feature grid. Feature grids are close to useless here, because nearly every app in this category ships the same handful of destinations. What actually separates them is fit. How many orders you do, how many storefronts you run, whether you have already built your life inside Google Tag Manager, and who opens the reporting on the first of the month.

So this is a ranking by store shape. I build one of these apps, so read the last section with suspicion. The seven before it are the real answer.

## Who actually needs a tracking app, and who is fine without one?

Plenty of stores are fine without one, and no vendor will tell you that.

- **You are probably fine as you are** if you run one ad channel, spend under a few thousand a month, and your customers buy on the same day they click. Shopify's native pixel integrations will get you close enough that a paid app cannot pay for itself.
- **You are probably losing data** if you sell something with a considered purchase cycle. Safari clears all script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without interaction on your site <Cite href="https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/">per WebKit's own documentation</Cite>. A customer who browses on Tuesday and buys a fortnight later has already been forgotten by your browser-side setup.
- **You are definitely losing data** if you run three or more channels and each one claims a different share of the same revenue. That is a deduplication problem, and it does not fix itself.
- **You have a structural constraint** if you are on checkout extensibility, because Shopify's Web Pixels API gives apps controlled APIs inside a Lax or Strict sandbox <Cite href="https://shopify.dev/docs/api/web-pixels-api">by design</Cite>. Scripts cannot simply read the page any more, and every app here works within that.

The honest test: open your ad platform, open your Shopify orders for the same window, and look at the gap. If the gap is small and stable, spend the money on creative instead. If the gap is large, or worse, unstable month to month, keep reading. The mechanics of why are covered in [server-side tracking on Shopify](/blog/shopify-server-side-tracking).

## How should you rank the best Shopify conversion tracking apps?

Four things decide this, and they are all facts about your store rather than facts about the software.

- **Order volume**, because pricing in this category is banded by orders and the cost per order swings wildly across the bands.
- **Storefront count**, because consolidating several stores or several currencies is a genuinely different engineering problem to instrumenting one.
- **Existing GTM commitment**, because if you have two years of container history, an app that ignores GTM is asking you to throw work away.
- **Who owns reporting**, because a merchant, an in-house analyst, and an agency want three different products.

What I am ignoring: integration counts, because everyone lists the same names. Dashboards, because you already have somewhere to read numbers. And attribution modelling claims, which are a different category of tool.

## Which app fits a store under 500 orders a month?

At this size, the correct instinct is to pay nothing, and the correct instinct is right more often than vendors would like.

Both Stape and Trackproof are free on the App Store <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/server-gtm">Stape</Cite> and <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/trackproof">Trackproof</Cite>. Littledata's Flex plan takes a different approach at $0.35 per order with no monthly floor <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/littledata">on its listing</Cite>, which is a fair deal at genuinely small volume and stops being one quickly.

<Example>
A store doing 120 orders a month on a per-order plan at $0.35 pays $42.

The same store at 400 orders pays $140. At 500 it is $175, roughly what a fixed entry plan costs elsewhere.

So per-order is attractive under about 200 orders and stops being interesting above 400. Substitute your own volume. The crossover point is the whole decision.
</Example>

The reason not to simply take the cheapest is match quality. An app that fires every event but hands the ad platform almost nothing to identify the customer with is worse than one that sends fewer, better-identified events. At low volume you have fewer conversions to work with, so each one matters more. This is usually the real reason Meta reports fewer purchases than your admin does, and it is not solved by spending more. The [difference between a browser pixel and a server-side event](/blog/server-side-tracking-vs-pixel) is where that gets fixed.

## Which app fits a multi-store or multi-currency merchant?

This is where I would tell you to pay, and where I would not recommend our app first.

Littledata and Elevar are the two I would shortlist, and both are priced for scale rather than for a first store. Littledata's Plus band is $990 a month for 10,000 orders <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/littledata">on the listing</Cite>. If you run five country storefronts feeding one P&L, you want one vendor holding the whole picture rather than five installs you reconcile by hand. Make whichever you pick prove it on your storefront count during the trial. Do not take my word for it, or theirs.

Multi-currency adds a second problem that no tracking app fully solves. Shopify's rounding keeps converted prices stable, requires Shopify Payments, and the rounding rules cannot be customised <Cite href="https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/markets/pricing/rounding">per Shopify's documentation</Cite>. That means the value your customer paid and the value your tracking sends can legitimately differ by small amounts, in every currency, forever.

- **Decide your reporting currency first.** Not the app's job. Yours.
- **Send the presentment currency and the value together.** An app that drops the currency code is giving the ad platform a number with no unit.
- **Expect small permanent discrepancies** from rounding, and do not spend a week hunting a bug that is actually a documented feature.

## Which app fits a store already committed to Google Tag Manager?

If you have a server-side GTM container that someone has spent real time on, the ranking inverts.

Stape is where I would start, and it is free <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/server-gtm">on the App Store</Cite>. It also carries the lowest rating of the five, 4.4 stars across 38 reviews <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/server-gtm">on that same listing</Cite>. Read that as a product that assumes competence rather than one that sends worse data. Free means you can test that assumption yourself in an afternoon instead of arguing about it.

Elevar is the paid alternative, listed on the App Store as a GTM data layer app, with an entry plan at $225 a month for 2,000 orders <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/gtm-datalayer-by-elevar">per its listing</Cite>.

Be honest about the commitment, though. "We use GTM" and "someone here can debug a server container at 9pm" are different statements. If it is the first one, the container is not an asset you are protecting. It is a habit. Apps that replace it entirely will be less work.

## Which app fits when an agency owns the reporting?

Different buyer, different product. The agency is not optimising for your monthly cost. It is optimising for time on setup, predictability across its client book, and the ability to hand over a working implementation.

Analyzify is where I would look first. It has the largest review base of the five, 4.7 stars over 272 reviews, and pricing starts at $145 a month for 5,000 orders <Cite href="https://apps.shopify.com/analyzify">on the App Store</Cite>. A big review base matters to an agency for a reason unrelated to accuracy. The setup path is well trodden, so whoever you hired has probably walked it before.

- **Ask who owns the account.** If the app is installed under the agency's login, you will discover the problem on the day you part ways.
- **Ask for the event specification in writing.** Event names, parameters, and identifiers. Anything undocumented becomes unmaintainable the moment staff change.
- **Insist on a channel-by-channel check.** A setup that nails Meta and quietly ignores TikTok is common, and TikTok has its own rule: the event ID has to be shared through both the Pixel and the Events API, and the first event received is the one kept <Cite href="https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/event-deduplication?lang=en">per TikTok</Cite>.

## What do App Store ratings hide about tracking apps specifically?

Ratings are useful for a theme app. They are close to meaningless for a tracking app, and here is why.

A review is written by someone who noticed something. With tracking, the failure mode is silence. Events that were never sent produce no error, no support ticket, and no one-star review. The merchant simply sees slightly worse ad performance and blames the algorithm. So the reviews you read describe onboarding and how fast someone replied on a Sunday. All real. None of it is evidence about data accuracy.

Second, review counts track age and install base. The numbers below tell you which products have sold longest, not which one gets your purchase values right.

## Where does Trackproof rank, and where does it not?

Trackproof is free and it is new. Those two facts explain most of where it sits.

It fits if you run one storefront, sell across several ad channels, and want server-side events with correct deduplication and no monthly bill to defend. It does not fit if you need consolidated reporting across several stores, a vendor with years of public track record, or someone to migrate an existing GTM build for you. In those three cases the answer is above, and it is not us.

Here is how I would actually decide, in order:

1. **Measure your gap first.** Compare one full week of ad-platform conversions against Shopify orders. Write the number down. Without it you cannot tell whether any app helped.
2. **Pick by shape, using the store-shape table above.** Order volume and storefront count knock out most of the field before you compare features at all.
3. **Install the free option first**, whichever it is. If free closes your gap, you have your answer and you keep the money.
4. **Run the old and new setup together for a week.** Match event IDs so the platforms deduplicate properly rather than double-counting your overlap.
5. **Re-measure the same gap, then decide.** If a paid app closes it further than a free one, the invoice is justified by a number rather than by a feature list.

That sequence costs you two weeks and nothing else. It is a better guide than any ranking, including this one.
